# Chapter 10: Outside the House

## Chapter purpose
Imogen locates the 1923 conveyance naming Celeste as purchaser. Adrian drives Claire to the county archive.

## Mystery movement
- **Clues:** C5
- **Red herrings:** R3
- Evidence must be observed before it is interpreted.
- POV inference cannot exceed the physical/documentary record.

## Romance / relationship movement
Respect deepens away from house

## End hook
Harcourt calls immediately after Adrian forwards the 1923 conveyance.

## Draft

Imogen Shaw called at 7:42 the next morning.

Claire was already awake.

“You sound pleased,” she said.

“I am never pleased before eight.”

“Then you found something.”

“I found several things, one of which may annoy a landed family.”

Claire sat up.

“That usually improves your mornings.”

“Immensely.”

Imogen’s keyboard clicked in the background.

“I pulled the county archive catalogue and an old deeds index for the Blackmere parcel. Western boundary along the quarry track. Conveyance completed August 1923.”

Claire reached for her notebook.

“Purchaser?”

A pause.

“Celeste Vane.”

Claire stopped writing.

“Not Sebastian Raven?”

“No.”

“Not trustees for the Raven family?”

“No.”

“Joint purchase?”

“No.”

Imogen’s voice sharpened.

“And before you ask, this is not a modern Land Registry title entry. The land was unregistered then. What I have is a catalogue entry for the conveyance itself. The archive holds a deposited deed packet, reference BLK/PROP/1923/441.”

Claire swung her feet to the floor.

“Can we access it today?”

“I called. Reading room has a slot at eleven-thirty. You’re welcome.”

“You called before telling me?”

“I know you.”

“Unfortunately.”

“Deeply.”

Claire looked towards the window.

Raven House was grey in morning light, its western roofline visible beyond wet glass.

For a week she had treated ownership as background. The house belonged to the Ravens now. Celeste had designed it. Those were separate propositions.

Maybe they had not always been separate.

“Imogen,” Claire said, “if Celeste bought the land—”

“Do not build the case before the paper.”

Claire smiled despite herself.

“Terrible habit.”

“I learned from you.”

By eight-thirty Adrian was in the library.

Claire slid the catalogue reference across the table.

“What is this?”

“A 1923 conveyance reference.”

“For Raven House?”

“For the Blackmere parcel on which Raven House was built.”

He read the note.

“Celeste Vane.”

“That is what the deeds index says.”

“Could she have bought it on Sebastian’s behalf?”

“Possibly. Could have transferred it later. Could have been acting under a trust we haven’t seen. Could be an imperfect catalogue description. We need the actual instrument.”

Adrian looked at the clock.

“When?”

“Eleven-thirty.”

“I’ll drive.”

“You don’t need to.”

“I know.”

That answer caught her.

He picked up his keys.

The county archive was forty-five minutes away, which meant forty-five minutes inside a car without Raven House mediating the conversation.

Claire discovered she preferred Adrian outside his own estate.

He drove an old dark-green estate car rather than anything theatrical. The heater worked too well. He listened to public radio quietly. At the first village he stopped for coffee without asking whether she wanted one and returned with two.

“You assumed.”

“You drank mine yesterday.”

“I accepted one you offered.”

“An aggressive distinction.”

She tasted it.

Correct order.

That was more unsettling than it should have been.

“Do you remember everyone’s coffee?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Adrian glanced at her.

“Was that a test?”

“Everything matters until it doesn’t.”

“Right. The creed.”

They drove another mile.

“Why architectural history?” he asked.

Claire looked out at stone walls moving past the window.

“Buildings are bad liars.”

“That sounds rehearsed.”

“It isn’t.”

“Why are they bad liars?”

“Because alteration leaves evidence. Even when people rewrite the paperwork, materials disagree. Mortar dates differently. Timber scars. Paint layers survive beneath later taste. Rooms are harder to persuade than witnesses.”

“And people?”

“People are excellent liars.”

He was quiet.

Claire regretted the sharpness.

Then Adrian said, “You say that as if you have peer-reviewed it.”

She laughed.

The archive occupied a converted civic building whose heating system had declared war on paper preservation.

Imogen waited at the entrance in a red scarf and the expression of a woman who had already corrected three forms.

She hugged Claire, then looked at Adrian.

“So you’re the house.”

Adrian blinked.

Claire said, “Imogen.”

“What? You describe him almost entirely through building problems.”

“I have never described him.”

“Exactly.”

Adrian held out a hand.

“Adrian Raven.”

“Dr Imogen Shaw. I specialise in archives, documentary law and embarrassing my friends.”

“I had gathered the third.”

Claire walked ahead.

The conveyance packet arrived in a grey archival box.

They worked in silence at first.

The outer wrapper was brittle but legible.

**Conveyance of land at Blackmere.**

Date: 14 August 1923.

Vendor: Thomas Bell & Sons, quarry proprietors.

Purchaser: **Miss Celeste Vane, architect, of 41 Fitzroy Square, London.**

Claire felt the click of evidence settling into place.

She photographed the wrapper, then the deed itself.

The operative clause was clear.

Consideration paid by Celeste.

Land conveyed to Celeste.

No Sebastian.

No Raven trustees.

No wording that she bought as agent for anyone else.

Adrian read the lines twice.

“Architect,” he said.

Claire looked at him.

“What?”

“It calls her architect.”

“Yes.”

“Family histories call her the designer.”

There was distaste in his voice.

Claire understood it.

Designer sounded decorative. Optional. A woman choosing fabrics around a Raven man’s vision.

Architect was authorship.

“It matters,” she said.

“I know.”

They continued through the packet.

A site sketch matched Raven House boundaries almost exactly. A note from Celeste’s solicitor referred to “Miss Vane’s proposed residence at Blackmere.”

Her residence.

Not the Raven estate.

Imogen leaned over the table.

“This tells us who acquired the legal estate in 1923. It does not tell us every later dealing.”

“There must be a transfer somewhere,” Adrian said.

“Maybe,” Imogen said. “And because this was unregistered land, that answer should live in the deed chain rather than in a 1923 register entry.”

Adrian looked at her.

Imogen added, “Do not panic. One conveyance is not a title opinion.”

“I’m not panicking.”

Claire said, “He does it inwardly.”

Adrian gave her a look.

Imogen smiled. “Excellent. I like him.”

Claire ignored both of them.

The final enclosure was a receipt.

Celeste had paid the purchase money from an account in her own name.

Claire photographed that too.

Adrian sat back.

“My grandfather always said Sebastian commissioned the house.”

“Commissioned and owned are different things,” Claire said.

“What if he paid for construction?”

“Then we establish that separately.”

“What if she conveyed it to him before she died?”

“Then the deed chain should show it.”

“And if it doesn’t?”

Claire met his eyes.

“Then the family story becomes legally interesting.”

Outside, they stood under the archive entrance while rain began again in fine diagonal lines.

Imogen had another appointment and left them with a promise to trace later conveyances and, separately, the date on which Raven House first entered the modern Land Register.

Adrian did not start the car immediately.

He rested both hands on the steering wheel.

“My father knew something.”

Claire turned towards him.

“Why do you say that?”

“He used to correct people when they called Celeste Sebastian’s architect.”

“What did he say?”

“That Sebastian was Celeste’s client.”

“Different emphasis.”

“I thought he was being pedantic.”

“Pedantry is often evidence with poor social skills.”

He smiled faintly.

Then the smile disappeared.

“I am selling a house I may not understand how we acquired.”

“Today you learned who bought the land. That is all.”

“You’re very good at refusing drama.”

“It tends to obscure sequence.”

He looked at her for a long moment.

“Does anything frighten you?”

“Yes.”

He waited.

Claire did not elaborate.

Adrian nodded once.

Boundary accepted.

Again.

On the drive back, they talked less but the silence had changed. It no longer felt like two people protecting separate territories. It felt companionable.

At Raven House, Claire carried the scan copies into the library. Adrian photographed the conveyance reference page with his phone, then forwarded the archive scans to Peter Harcourt with one sentence:

**Please tell me where the title leaves Celeste Vane.**

Harcourt called before Adrian had put the phone down.

His voice was faint but urgent through the handset.

“What exactly have you obtained?”

Adrian looked through the open library door at Claire.

“The 1923 conveyance.”

A pause.

Then Harcourt said something that made Adrian’s expression close.

“Yes,” Adrian replied. “Claire Mercer requested it.”

Another pause.

“No, Peter. I am not speculating about title. I am asking you to trace the deeds.”

Claire set the document box down slowly.

Adrian listened.

Then said, “Why does that concern you?”

The call had stopped being about history.

Peter Harcourt was worried about the active sale.

And whatever sat between a 1923 conveyance to Celeste Vane and the modern Raven title, he wanted it handled by lawyers before anyone else gave it a name.
